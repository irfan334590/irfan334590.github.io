A researcher working where biomedical AI meets clinical reality.

Positioning

I am a Senior Researcher, Principal Investigator, Visiting Research Fellow, and Supervisor in artificial intelligence for healthcare. My work spans AI for health monitoring, prevention, intervention and treatment, together with translational medical imaging and wearable physiological sensing.

I completed my first PhD in Biomedical Engineering at Fudan University in December 2024 and my second PhD in Information and Communication Engineering at the University of Turku in April 2026. The two degrees shaped the same research direction from two sides: biomedical measurement and computational efficiency.

The strongest version of this profile is not “AI for healthcare” in general. It is more specific: translating multimodal sleep, cardiovascular, signal, and imaging biomarkers into deployable AI systems for dementia care, CVD risk, and personalised monitoring.

Research thesis

Healthcare AI should not only win benchmarks. It should be computationally efficient, privacy-aware, interpretable enough for clinical reasoning, and robust enough for longitudinal home and hospital data. That is why my work combines edge AI, federated learning, wearable physiology, and translational imaging.

Research map

A clearer way to understand the portfolio: sensors and imaging generate biomarkers; AI models turn them into risk, prognosis and monitoring; edge/federated deployment moves them toward real-world care.

Wearable physiology

ECG, PPG, EEG, EMG, GSR, actigraphy, Polar 360 and Polar AccessLink pipelines for continuous home monitoring.

Medical imaging

MRI, CT, PET and ultrasound models for brain tumours, stroke, head-and-neck cancer and cerebrovascular stratification.

Clinical outcomes

Dementia care, cardiovascular disease, pain, anxiety, depression, quality of life, cognitive decline and treatment toxicity.

1 · AcquireSignals, images and clinical variables from hospital and home settings.
2 · LearnEfficient CNNs, ensembles, foundation models, transfer learning and multimodal fusion.
3 · ProtectFederated and edge–cloud workflows to reduce data transfer and support privacy.
4 · TranslateDashboards, risk models and caregiver/clinician decision-support pathways.

Heart–brain–sleep axis

Connecting cardiovascular dysfunction, sleep biomarkers, dementia risk, stroke and cognitive decline.

Efficient AI

LiteML architectures, lightweight CNNs and hybrid models designed for practical edge–cloud deployment.

Care impact

Monitoring, risk stratification, adaptive interventions, caregiver support and personalised treatment planning.

Teacher for Statistical Data Analysis at the University of Turku, serving approximately 150 undergraduate and graduate students. Teaching uses biomedical datasets and Python/Jupyter notebooks to connect statistics, machine learning and health technology.

The important message here is not only that teaching happened, but that teaching is research-led: students work with problems that resemble real biomedical AI pipelines.
